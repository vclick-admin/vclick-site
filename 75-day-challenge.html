75-day challenge

Updated 22 November 2026 · 5 min read

A 75-day challenge is a short list of daily rules you keep for 75 days in a row: a workout, a rule about food, a few pages of a book. Here’s a tracker to print, ideas for rules, and a quick way to see when your day 75 falls.

Many 75-day challenges online come with a fixed list of rules. The tracker here has blank lines, so the rules are yours.

Printable 75-day tracker

Write the challenge name, your dates and up to five rules at the top. Tick a square only on days when you kept every rule.

When does day 75 fall?

75 days is ten weeks and five days. Day 1 is the day you start, so if you start on Friday, January 1, 2027, day 75 is Tuesday, March 16.

How to set your rules

  • Three to five rules. Each extra rule is one more way to miss a day. If you’re not sure, start with three.
  • Yes or no in the evening. “Read 10 pages” is easy to check. “Read more” isn’t.
  • Plan for the worst day of the 75. A trip, a cold, a late night at work. If a rule only works on a free evening, make it smaller.
  • Build rest into the rule. For example, “30 minutes of exercise, and one day a week it can be a walk.” Over 75 days your body needs easier days.
  • Decide what a missed day means. The strict rule, common in 75-day challenges, is to start again from day 1. The gentler one is to circle the day and carry on. Pick one before you start.

If 75 days feels like too much, start with a 30-day challenge.

Rule ideas

Movement

  • 30 minutes of exercise: a workout, a run, a swim or a long walk.
  • 8,000 steps, checked on your phone before bed.
  • 10 minutes of stretching after you get up.
  • Stairs instead of the elevator, every time.

Food and drink

  • No alcohol.
  • No soda or energy drinks.
  • No takeout or delivery.
  • Vegetables with lunch and dinner.
  • Nothing to eat after 8 pm.

Mind

  • 10 pages of a book.
  • 10 minutes of meditation.
  • A page in a journal before bed.
  • 15 minutes of a new language.

Phone, sleep and money

  • No social media before noon.
  • The phone stays out of the bedroom.
  • In bed by 11.
  • Every expense written down the same day.

Four ready-made sets

If you’d rather not build your own, take one of these and adjust it.

  • Getting started: a 20-minute walk, no soda, 10 pages of a book.
  • Fitness: 30 minutes of exercise (one day a week it can be a walk), vegetables with lunch and dinner, no alcohol, 8,000 steps.
  • Less phone: no social media before noon, the phone stays out of the bedroom, 15 minutes of something offline, like reading, drawing or an instrument.
  • Money: no takeout, nothing bought beyond groceries and bills, every expense written down the same day.

With friends

Agree on the same rules and the same start date, and post a tick in a group chat each evening. Decide together what happens when someone misses a day, before anyone does.

Why 75 days

In a study at University College London, it took the typical participant 66 days before a new daily action felt automatic, and the range ran from 18 to 254 days. So 75 days is enough time for many people, though not for everyone. If a rule still takes real effort on day 75, keep going.

If you haven’t exercised in a while, start with walks and build up. If you have a health condition or you’re pregnant, talk to a doctor before taking on a daily workout or a strict food rule.
