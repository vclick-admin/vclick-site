Sober January 2027

Updated 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

No alcohol from 1 to 31 January. This page has a free printable calendar for January 2027, the rules worth settling before day one, and what helps on the evenings when it’s hard.

The best-known version of this month is Dry January®, a campaign the UK charity Alcohol Change UK has run since 2013. Many people in the US, Canada and Ireland take January off alcohol on their own and call it Sober January. The calendar below works for either.

Decide these before 1 January

  • Alcohol-free drinks. Beer and wine sold as alcohol-free or non-alcoholic can still contain a little alcohol, up to 0.5% depending on the country. Decide whether they count for you.
  • What you’ll drink instead. Keep something in the fridge for the moments you’d usually open a bottle: sparkling water with lime, ginger beer, alcohol-free beer if you’ve decided it’s fine.
  • What you’ll say. “I’m doing a month off” is usually enough. If you’d rather not explain, a glass of something else in your hand ends most offers.
  • What counts as a slip, and what happens next. The strict rule is to start the count again from day one. The gentler one is to carry on and circle that day on the calendar. Pick one now, so you don’t have to decide on the night.

The hard moments

In 2027, 1 January falls on a Friday, so the first weekend comes straight away. After that, the tricky evenings tend to be the same ones every week: Friday after work, dinners out, birthdays.

  • Look at your January plans now and mark the evenings where you’d normally drink. Plan each one: what you’ll order and when you’ll leave.
  • Tell the people you usually drink with. It’s easier when they know before the first round.
  • Plan something for Friday evenings that doesn’t happen in a bar: a film, a long walk, cooking something new.
  • Bring your own drink to parties, so you’re not holding a glass of tap water all night.

Mark every day

A calendar on the fridge works because you see it every time you open the door. The longer the row of ticks, the harder it is to break.

After January

Some people go back to drinking as before. Others keep a rule or two from the month, like no alcohol on weeknights. If you want to keep going, set a longer challenge: 60 or 100 days.

If you drink every day and get shaky, sweaty or anxious when you stop, don’t stop suddenly. For people who are physically dependent on alcohol, stopping all at once can be dangerous. Talk to a doctor first: they can help you cut down safely.
