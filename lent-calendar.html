Lent 2027 calendar

Updated 1 November 2026 · 6 min read

Lent 2027 starts on Ash Wednesday, February 10, and ends with Easter on March 28. Below is a calendar from Ash Wednesday to Easter to print and put on the fridge. The 40 days are already counted, and there’s room at the top for what you’re giving up.

Lent 2027 dates

  • Shrove Tuesday (Mardi Gras, Pancake Day): February 9, the day before Lent.
  • Ash Wednesday: February 10, day 1.
  • First Sunday of Lent: February 14, which is also Valentine’s Day.
  • Palm Sunday: March 21, the start of Holy Week.
  • Holy Thursday (Maundy Thursday): March 25.
  • Good Friday: March 26.
  • Holy Saturday: March 27, day 40.
  • Easter Sunday: March 28.

For Catholics, Ash Wednesday and Good Friday are days of fasting (from age 18 to 59) and no meat (from age 14), and every Friday of Lent is meat-free.

Most Orthodox churches still calculate Easter by the old Julian calendar, so their Lent usually comes later. In 2027 Great Lent starts on Clean Monday, March 15, and Orthodox Easter is on May 2.

“Day 1” to “Day 40” in the corners count the days of Lent. Sundays don’t get a number, and the next section explains why. Print at 100% (Actual size) so the squares stay big.

Why 40 days, and do Sundays count?

From Ash Wednesday to Holy Saturday there are 46 days. Leave out the six Sundays and you get 40, the number of days Jesus fasted in the desert. Sundays are still part of Lent, but they aren’t days of fasting, so the count skips them.

Whether you keep your promise on Sundays is your call. The US Catholic bishops say personal Lent sacrifices are a matter of individual conscience, and people do it both ways. Decide before Ash Wednesday and write it under “My rules”: by week three it’s harder to be fair with yourself.

In the Catholic calendar, the season of Lent itself ends at sundown on Holy Thursday. The 40-day count still runs through Holy Saturday, so the calendar goes all the way to Easter.

What to give up for Lent

Pick one thing and write the rule so you can check it with a yes or no every evening. “Eat less sugar” is hard to check. “No chocolate or desserts, fruit is fine” is easy.

  • Chocolate and sweets. Decide now whether that includes birthday cake at work. Valentine’s Day falls on the first Sunday of Lent, so your Sunday rule gets tested in week one.
  • Alcohol. St. Patrick’s Day is on Wednesday, March 17, day 31. Tips for the hard evenings are on the Sober January page.
  • Social media. Delete the apps from your phone until Easter. If you need one for work, use it on a computer.
  • Coffee. The first few days can bring headaches, so some people cut down during the week before Ash Wednesday.
  • Takeout and delivery. You cook, or you sit down to eat out.
  • Meat. Catholics go without it on the Fridays of Lent anyway. This takes it to every day until Easter.
  • Online shopping. Only things that run out: food, medicine, toothpaste.
  • The snooze button. Up on the first alarm.
  • Complaining. Every complaint puts a dollar in a jar, and the jar goes to charity at Easter.

What to take on instead

Christian tradition describes Lent through three practices: prayer, fasting and almsgiving, which means giving to people in need. Giving something up is the fasting part. If you’d rather add something, these are easy to check each evening:

  • A chapter of the Gospels a day. Mark and Luke together have exactly 40 chapters, one for each day of Lent.
  • Ten minutes of quiet in the morning before you look at your phone: prayer, or just sitting still.
  • A dollar or a pound a day in a jar. At Easter, give it to a food bank or a charity you choose.
  • One thing out of the house each day. By Easter that’s 40 things donated or thrown away.
  • A walk every day, at least 30 minutes.
  • Write to one person a week. A letter or a long message to someone you’ve lost touch with: six weeks, six people.

Getting to day 40

Lent has fixed dates, so a slip doesn’t send you back to day one. Circle that day on the calendar and carry on tomorrow.

Look at your February and March now and mark the days that will be hard: birthdays, dinners out, a work trip. Decide for each one what you’ll do. It also helps when someone knows. Tell a friend, or do Lent together with the same calendar on each fridge.
