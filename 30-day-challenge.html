A 30-day challenge is one small thing you do every day for a month: a walk, ten pages of a book, no sugar. Here’s a tracker to print, 30 ideas with rules that are easy to check, and what to do when you miss a day.

Printable 30-day tracker

One sheet per challenge. Write the name, the start and finish dates and your rules, then cross off a square each evening. There’s a line at the bottom for notes.

How to pick a challenge

  • One at a time. Add a second challenge once the first one runs without effort.
  • A rule you can check. “Walk 20 minutes” you can check in the evening. “Move more” you can’t.
  • Small enough for a bad day. Ten pages on a tired Tuesday are still ten pages. A rule that only works on good days won’t last 30 of them.
  • Write down what happens if you miss a day. It’s harder to be fair with yourself on day 17.

30-day challenge ideas

Food and sleep

  • No added sugar. No desserts, soda or sweetened coffee. Fruit is fine.
  • No alcohol. Not a glass, weekends included. Tips for the hard evenings are on the Sober January page.
  • Cook dinner at home. No takeout or delivery.
  • Vegetables with every lunch. Even if it’s just a salad or a carrot.
  • Nothing to eat after 8 pm. Water and unsweetened tea are fine.
  • In bed by 11. The phone charges in another room.

Movement

  • A 30-minute walk. Outside, without stopping for coffee.
  • 10,000 steps. Your phone or watch counts them. Check the number before bed.
  • Plank. Start at 20 seconds and add 5 each day. On day 30 that’s 2 minutes 45 seconds.
  • 50 squats. Split them through the day if you like.
  • 10 minutes of stretching in the morning, before coffee and before your phone.
  • A mile a day. Run it, or run and walk. Speed doesn’t matter.

Mind

  • Ten pages a day. That’s 300 pages by day 30, about one book.
  • A language, 15 minutes a day. An app lesson, a show without subtitles, a conversation.
  • Ten minutes of meditation. Sit, follow your breath, set a timer.
  • Three things you’re grateful for, written down each evening.
  • A page in a journal before bed, about anything.
  • One photo a day. By day 30 you have the month in 30 pictures.

Money

  • No-spend month. Only essentials: groceries, bills, medicine, getting to work.
  • Track every expense the same day, coffee included.
  • Save a dollar more each day. $1 on day 1, $30 on day 30: $465 in total.

Phone

  • No social media before noon.
  • The phone sleeps outside the bedroom. Use an alarm clock.
  • No phone at meals, at home or out.

Home

  • One thing out of the house each day. Donate it, sell it or throw it away: 30 things by the end.
  • Make your bed as soon as you get up.

Couples and families

  • Dinner without phones. Phones stay in another room while you eat.
  • A walk together, at least 15 minutes in the evening.
  • Call your parents or grandparents, even for five minutes.
  • Read to your kids before bed: one story or one chapter.

If you miss a day

Decide this before day 1, while it’s easy to be strict. The strict rule is to start again from day 1. The gentler one is to circle the missed day and carry on. A common middle ground: one missed day is allowed, two in a row means starting over.

Is 30 days enough to build a habit?

Often not quite. In a study at University College London, 96 volunteers each picked one daily action, like a glass of water with lunch or a run before dinner. For the typical participant it took 66 days before the action felt automatic, and the range ran from 18 to 254 days. Simple actions stuck faster than exercise.

A 2024 review of 20 studies found much the same: about two months for a typical person, with big differences between people. So treat 30 days as a test run. If you want to keep the habit, carry on to 60 or 100 days.
